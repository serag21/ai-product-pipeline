# Little Haunts Club — Art Direction and QA Plan

## Research-led direction

This is an original collection, not a reproduction of competitor pages. Competitor research informed only category-level decisions: a mixed cast of instantly recognizable spooky archetypes; a cozy/comedic mood; and a readable small story supported by a fireplace, frame, flowers, pumpkins, tombstones, windows, and similar contextual objects.

Sources consulted:
- Black Forest Labs, [FLUX.2 Prompting Guide](https://docs.bfl.ai/guides/prompting_guide_flux2): lead with subject and action, then critical style, then context; wording order matters; describe intended output affirmatively.
- Penguin Random House, [Spooky Cutie](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/824611/spooky-cutie-by-coco-wyo/): current ages 7+ listing positions a varied spooky cast and large bold designs.
- Etsy, [Printable Halloween Creatures](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1305339872/printable-halloween-coloring-pages): mixed Halloween character types can be the offer, instead of a repetitive ghost-and-cat collection.
- Etsy, [Haunted Home Moments](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4337130396/haunted-home-moments-coloring-book-cozy) and [Spooky Comfy](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4396516924/spooky-comfy-cozy-creepy-coloring-book): current sample imagery illustrates varied archetypes, cozy environments, and prop-led storytelling.

The final compositions and character descriptions are original. We do not copy a competitor's page layout, characters, or specific drawings.

## Why v2 is not the release candidate

The current 17-page candidate book shows cleaner outlines than earlier tests, but the pack has not yet earned launch approval:
- The same ghost-and-cat pairing appears too often.
- Floating black bats are repeated as background filler across many pages.
- Some nighttime windows and the television frame generate large filled-black regions, which remove useful coloring area.
- The candy-sorting page has confusing anthropomorphic cat anatomy and unclear candy handoff.
- The pumpkin-patch page uses dense hay and vine textures that crowd the main character.
- Several scenes have props with weak story function or no distinct focal gag.

These issues require a new composition and character plan, not threshold cleanup alone.

## Individual page intent and review

### 01. Ghost Tailor Repairs a Bat Cape

**Main character:** Friendly sheet ghost  
**Why this scene belongs:** Keeps the strongest existing ghost-tailor idea, but gives the black cat a large colorable body and a clear reason to be in the scene. The sewing machine and framed example communicate the activity immediately.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check that the sewing machine sits fully on the table, scissors rest flat, cat paws meet the floor, and the frame stays separate from the cape.

### 02. Patchwork Monster Builds a Birdhouse

**Main character:** Original stitched monster child  
**Why this scene belongs:** Introduces the Frankenstein-like archetype in an original garden-building scene rather than copying a competitor's bird-and-nest composition. The birdhouse, tools, bird, pumpkins, and flowers each serve the central action.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check exactly two neck bolts, clear birdhouse/post contact, hammer held naturally, bird feet touching the roof, and broad uncluttered overalls.

### 03. The Plague-Doctor Crow's Apothecary

**Main character:** Friendly crow in a beaked doctor mask  
**Why this scene belongs:** Adds the distinctive masked-bird character called out in the reference, with a friendly, non-graphic presentation. The apothecary has a clear role and the props form a coherent counter display.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check the mask reads as one beak-shaped costume, the crow has one consistent pair of wings and feet, bottles sit flat, herbs attach to the rail, and the background window stays white.

### 04. Skeleton's Fireside Story Hour

**Main character:** Cozy childlike skeleton  
**Why this scene belongs:** Delivers the exact cozy indoor richness we want: fireplace, framed wall art, a mug, cookies, rug, and a clear character activity. It also introduces a skeleton without leaning on a graveyard.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check armchair legs touch the floor, hands hold the book naturally, fire stays inside the firebox, portrait is attached to the wall, and skeleton bones remain readable and colorable.

### 05. Mummy's Tangled Present

**Main character:** Friendly mummy  
**Why this scene belongs:** Uses the mummy's defining bandages as the joke itself, rather than adding bandage texture everywhere. The gift-wrapping tools and parcels all support one simple action.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check the loose bandage connects continuously from the mummy to the ribbon, both presents rest on the floor, the scissors are recognizable, and bandage strips remain broad.

### 06. The Witch's Tiny Potion Clinic

**Main character:** Friendly young witch  
**Why this scene belongs:** Gives the witch a clear, active magical task and uses familiar Halloween props in a grounded cottage scene. The labels are blank and the ingredients are large instead of becoming tiny pseudo-text.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check cauldron sits on the hearth, shelf brackets connect to wall and shelf, frog sits on stool, spoon connects to the witch's hand, and bubbles are outlined rather than filled.

### 07. Vampire's Midnight Bakery

**Main character:** Friendly young vampire baker  
**Why this scene belongs:** Combines a recognizable vampire silhouette and light humor with a bakery layout that makes physical sense. The cookie shapes add Halloween identity without filling the page with bats.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check tray is supported by the oven or held with both hands, oven door and rails make sense, cape remains white inside, and the starry window has a white background.

### 08. Ghost-and-Cat Blanket-Fort Night

**Main character:** Friendly ghost and curious cat  
**Why this scene belongs:** Keeps the strongest shared-character moment from the earlier batch. The fort's supports are visible, and each extra object reinforces a cozy movie/story-night activity.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check blanket contacts both chair backs, chair legs meet the floor, book has two clear pages, flashlight lies flat, and popcorn stays in the bowl.

### 09. The Skeleton Mermaid's Shell Collection

**Main character:** Friendly skeleton mermaid  
**Why this scene belongs:** Adds a clearly different creature and setting, drawing on the genre's spooky-mermaid appeal while keeping the anatomy stylized and colorable. The shells and treasure chest have a clear shared purpose.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check one head, one rib cage, two arms, one continuous tail, fish separated from the body, and treasure chest resting on the ledge.

### 10. Zombie's Pumpkin-Pie Stand

**Main character:** Friendly little zombie baker  
**Why this scene belongs:** Creates an outdoor market scene and a different character silhouette. The cart, pies, sign panel, awning, pumpkins, and crow all belong to the same small seasonal business.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check both wheels touch the ground, cart counter supports pies, crow feet touch handle, awning connects to cart, and zombie hands hold or rest on the counter clearly.

### 11. Scarecrow's First Pumpkin Harvest

**Main character:** Friendly patchwork scarecrow  
**Why this scene belongs:** Introduces a scarecrow and garden setting with a clear seasonal action. Limiting straw to a few large tufts avoids the dense scratchy texture that hurt the previous pumpkin-patch image.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check exactly three pumpkins, attached stems, crow feet touching the fence, fence rails connecting posts, and sparse straw detail.

### 12. Werewolf Pup's Moonlit Doghouse

**Main character:** Cute werewolf puppy  
**Why this scene belongs:** Adds a dog-like monster with a distinct body and simple emotional gag. The bowl, toy, house, and moon all support the puppy story without copying the ghost-and-cat format.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check four paws, one tail, doghouse doorway, roof supports, bowl and bone on the ground, and moon/stars as open outlined shapes.

### 13. Little Grim Reaper's Flower Delivery

**Main character:** Small friendly grim reaper  
**Why this scene belongs:** Adds the recognizable reaper archetype while making the story gentle and kind: bringing flowers rather than frightening anyone. The tombstone, gate, lantern, flowers, and pumpkin establish the place intentionally.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check both hands connect to bouquet, scythe leans against the post rather than floating, lantern attaches to gate, tombstone rests on ground, and flowers have clear stems.

### 14. Cyclops Runs the Pumpkin Ring Toss

**Main character:** Friendly one-eyed carnival monster  
**Why this scene belongs:** Adds a new monster silhouette and a clear activity-based scene. Targets, rings, prizes, booth, and pennants all reinforce a small Halloween carnival without relying on text.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check one eye centered on the face, ring actually encircles a pumpkin target, targets rest on shelf, plush sits on shelf, and awning has clear supports.

### 15. The Bat Courier's Moonlit Delivery

**Main character:** Small bat messenger  
**Why this scene belongs:** Uses a bat as the featured character instead of scattering identical filled silhouettes across every page. The delivery action, satchel, letter, mailbox, doorway, lantern, and flowers form one readable mini-story.  
**Full-resolution review focus:** Check one bat with two wings and two feet, wing panels stay open white, satchel attaches to body, mailbox is mounted to its post, porch light is attached to frame, and letter remains separate from feet and satchel.


## Shared release gate

A page is rejected or regenerated if its main character is malformed, limbs/props intersect nonsensically, a large area becomes solid black, important objects float, the scene does not communicate its activity, multiple tiny lines reduce coloring usability, or its silhouette/style feels materially different from the collection. A tool-generated PASS is only image-integrity QA; human visual QA decides sale readiness.
